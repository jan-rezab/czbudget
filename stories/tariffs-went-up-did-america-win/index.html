Data story / Trade & tariffs

Tariffs went up. Did America come out ahead?

Seven charts follow implemented tariffs, car imports, reciprocal duties and the cash the U.S. government actually kept.

Public Spending Data · 20 September 2026 · 7 min read · English

Editorial snapshot · Trade: July 2026 · Cash: August 2026 · Rates: 8 September 2026. Not a live tracker.

01 / What changed

The tariff came down from its peak.
It is still much higher than before.

Take a Chinese-made gasoline car with a 1.5–3.0 litre engine. The U.S. tariff estimate started at 7.5%, reached 52.5%, then fell to 32.5%.

On the same $30,000 import, that means
$2,250 before → $9,750 now.A $7,500 increase in calculated duty—not a prediction of the retail price.

Source: WTO–IMF tariff data. This is one car category, not every Chinese car. The estimate includes underlying duties and preferences.

02 / What crossed the border

For these cars: fewer from Canada and Mexico.
More from Europe and China.

For that same car category, the number entering the U.S. moved in different directions. There was no uniform collapse—or boom.

This is what happened, not proof of why. Prices, demand, production schedules and other factors also affect trade. Our extract starts after the initial 2025 tariff increases.

03 / The other side

“Reciprocal” does not mean equal.

For the same type of car, China and Canada charge more on U.S. imports than the U.S. charges in return. For Europe and Mexico, the pattern is reversed.

Source: WTO–IMF tariff data. All applied duties are included; not every duty is a retaliatory measure.

04 / The government’s money

Collections rose sharply.
Net revenue barely rose.

Across all goods and trading partners, the U.S. collected $292.5 billion in customs duties through August. But it also paid $125.2 billion in refunds.

The net result: +1.3% year on year.$167.3bn versus $165.2bn. More gross collections did not mean a comparable increase in net revenue.

Source: U.S. Treasury, Monthly Treasury Statement. All customs duties, not just the new tariffs.

The honest conclusion

A higher trade barrier.
Not a proven victory.

These figures show higher tariffs, uneven trade flows and a small increase in net customs revenue. They do not tell us whether gains for protected businesses outweighed costs to buyers, exporters and firms using imported parts.

What this story can—and cannot—tell you
  • The car example is HS2022 product 870323: passenger vehicles with only a spark-ignition engine, over 1,500 and no more than 3,000cc. It is not the whole automotive industry.
  • Rates are WTO best-available applied-rate estimates. An individual shipment’s legal bill depends on origin, product detail and applicable exemptions. The $30,000 example holds customs value fixed.
  • Physical counts and trade values are different measures. Counts use the same reported unit (vehicles); estimates are flagged. EU totals aggregate its 27 members without adding a separate World total.
  • The trade comparison uses October 2025 and July 2026, not a pre-tariff baseline. It cannot isolate seasonality or a causal tariff effect.
  • The receipts comparison is fiscal year to date, October through August. It is national cash accounting, not a measure of economic welfare or foreign payments to the U.S.

The evidence behind this edition

Published and extracted 20 September 2026. This is a frozen editorial edition, not a live tariff tracker. The evidence ends on different dates: trade in July, Treasury cash in August, and tariff-rate observations carried forward to 8 September.

Trade and applied rates

UN Comtrade: U.S. reporter 842, HS2022 870323 for the car example, imports and exports by month. Monthly all-goods comparisons cover Canada, China, EU27 and Mexico only. WTO–IMF tariff snapshots: U.S. direction e29a94eb-ccd4-4556-b822-beb8e9079cc0; reverse direction f119c988-708e-451f-bde6-dc50db48d9ef. Effective dates describe implemented measures, not announced intentions. Applied rates are estimates, not shipment-level legal advice.

Government cash

Inspect Treasury’s August 2026 Customs Duties row (JSON). MTS Table 4 reports gross receipts, refunds and net receipts. The same-period YTD comparison sums October–August in each fiscal year; it is not a trailing 12-month measure. Latest row verified against Treasury before publication.
