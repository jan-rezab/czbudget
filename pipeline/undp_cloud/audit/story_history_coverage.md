# HDR2025 history and story coverage

The ledger covers all 86 original caption objects: 67 numbered figures, six box figures, six tables and seven annex tables. Of these, 19 are conceptual and 67 contain statistical evidence. At least one published source receipt is bound to 43 statistical objects; this does not mean their exact original calculations have been reproduced. The other 24 have no admitted original numeric-source binding, including the two PISA objects currently held in private staging.

The verified report artifact is private release `369c3550-0253-40c7-9cf8-8b1acf944b17`. This audit publishes nothing. Its companion JSON records original source URLs, immutable releases and hashes, history scope, methodology, missing access and each original reference.

## Corrections before reconstruction

[UNDP’s official errata](https://hdr.undp.org/errata-and-corrigenda-hdr-2025) correct the O.4 workforce/non-workforce computations as of 6 May 2025. They also correct the period in figures 4.2 and 4.3 to **2000–2020**, as of 23 May 2025. The attached PDF has the older patent labels.

The survey statistics require different calculations. O.1/1.1 averages three domain percentages. Figure 1.3 counts likely or very likely productivity responses. Figure 6.3 additionally assigns half the neutral responses to the expected effect. Figure 2.1 uses control scores 8–10; figures O.6/4.4 use the future-minus-current difference in those shares.

For PISA 6.9, high critical thinking means level 4 or higher in all three subjects; the middle group reaches that level in at least one but not all three. Figure 6.10 uses at least one subject and equal country weights. Official HDI group aggregates combine weighted components, while figure 6.8 uses the mean of country changes.

## A six-chapter story

### 1. Human progress and the gap between AI use and expectation

Start with the full official HDI history and the regional slowdown. Then compare actual use with anticipated use across development groups, followed by use and productivity confidence across ages. Explain the historical cost of computing and the worker experiment as separate, bounded research findings.

Original references: O.2, O.3, 1.2, O.1, 1.1, 1.3, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8.

### 2. Choice as tools become agents

Compare current high perceived control with expected control in five years. Show the age gradient, then cultural alignment of model responses once the original experiment is recovered. Expectations describe perceptions, not observed future outcomes.

Original references: 2.1, O.6, 4.4, O.8, 2.4, 2.2, 2.3.

### 3. Access and learning across the life course

Move from corrected life-stage use patterns to internet access history and older people’s access gaps. Keep US assessment trends, the longitudinal cortical study, the mental-wellbeing age cross-sections and the BRFSS despair history distinct.

Original references: 3.1, Box3.3/F1, Box3.3/F2, 3.4, 3.5, 3.9, 3.10, 3.11, S3.1.1, S3.1.2, S3.1.4, 3.6.

### 4. Inclusion requires more than a connection

Pair disability access gaps with the corrected 2000–2020 assistive-patent geography. Show women’s share of all STEM graduates across the available years. Add the care-related ICT skills comparison only when the original household estimator is recovered.

Original references: 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.5, 4.6, Box4.1/F1.

### 5. AI capacity and decision power

Explain the four supply-chain snapshots using their separate periods and denominators. Follow with full model-release history, talent migration and skills penetration. Keep each composite index version distinct, and close with the original deliberation experiment.

Original references: 5.1, 5.5, 5.6, 5.7, 5.8, Table 5.2, 5.4.

### 6. Augmentation, creativity and planetary limits

Compare employment-weighted exposure with survey expectations. Present scientific disruption and Go creativity as original study findings with their methodological limits. Connect skills history and PISA learning patterns to temperature, material-footprint and emissions histories without attributing all pressures to AI.

Original references: 6.1, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4, 6.5, 6.6, 6.7, 6.8, 6.9, 6.10, 6.11, S6.1.1, Annex Table 7.

## Acceptance for a reconstructed chart

Each statistical panel needs a pinned source release and hash, all available source years, native units, the exact geography and denominator, filters and estimator, a benchmark against the published values and documented missing codes. Calculations are labelled calculations. Fixed experiments do not receive invented annual histories. Country histories retain missing years without interpolation.

Original and newer versions remain separate. Macro exports retain every native historical row, reported precision and null cell. Country series can load on demand without truncating those exports. A current retrieval date is not a real-time observation. A public article with zero normalized numeric records is not a loaded figure dataset.

The scientific disruption panel must mention the [2026 methodological challenge published by Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10787-y). The publisher’s metadata was verified; the full article could not be fetched here, so the ledger does not claim to have resolved that debate.

This inventory covers labelled objects, not every quantitative sentence in the 324-page report. Narrative evidence is tracked separately in the chapter source audits.
